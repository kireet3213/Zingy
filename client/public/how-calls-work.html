Zingy · video calls

How a WebRTC call works

WebRTC sends audio and video straight between two browsers. Before it can, the browsers have to find each other, agree on what to send and find a network path. This bench walks through every step of a Zingy call, in the order ConversationContainer.tsx runs it.

How to read the bench

The parts of the bench map one to one onto WebRTC. The lit part is whatever is moving at that moment.

The call in four stages

  1. Signal. The browsers can't reach each other yet, so they talk through the Zingy server: ring, accept, then the SDP offer and answer, which say what each side will send (audio, camera, screen) and how it will be encrypted.
  2. Gather. Each browser lists the addresses it might be reached at (host, STUN, TURN) and trickles them to the other side as new-ice-candidate messages.
  3. Connect. ICE sends small checks straight at the peer, best pair first. If a NAT drops them, the call falls back to the TURN relay.
  4. Stream. DTLS sets up the keys and SRTP carries the media on the chosen path. From then on the server only handles screen-share-state and video-hangup.
